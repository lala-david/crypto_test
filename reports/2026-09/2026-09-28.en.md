### 2. DAOstack — Hack / Exploit

- **Source**: [DAOstack — permissionless newOrganization lets an attacker hijack an existing Reputation contract and drain the Avatar](https://github.com/SunWeb3Sec/DeFiHackLabs/blob/main/src/test/2026-09/DAOstack_exp.sol) (DeFiHackLabs, 2026-09-26)
- **Incident date**: 2026-09-26  |  **Chains**: Ethereum  |  **Amount**: 4.025 ETH (full Genesis Alpha treasury); attacker minted 20,000 REP against the victim's real Reputation contract (supply 9,738.6 to 29,738.6, ~67% majority after a 200 REP GenesisProtocol vote-cost burn)
- **Tags**: defihacklabs
- **Known blacklist re-hits**: 1 (crimial_hunter: defihacklabs)

**Background**  
DAOstack’s governance uses a shared UController that owns the Reputation and Avatar contracts. The newOrganization function is permissionless and accepts any caller-supplied avatar without checking existing governance. This design flaw allows an attacker to bind the victim’s Reputation to a newly created organization.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
The permissionless newOrganization function lets the attacker hijack an existing Reputation contract and drain the Avatar’s ETH. By registering a fake avatar, the attacker gains a full-permission scheme and calls sendEther to withdraw the treasury.

**Summary**  
On Sep 26 2026 the attacker used the permissionless newOrganization call to hijack DAOstack’s Genesis Alpha DAO Reputation. He minted 20,000 REP, obtained a majority, and registered himself as a full-permission scheme via SchemeRegistrar. He then called sendEther to drain the Avatar’s full 4.025 ETH. The exploit transaction is 0xcfff7b060a7fb881f4bd8d2498dbe4e09454f7afaf5328fc12b59812f6737cc1.

**Fund Flow**  
Attacker contract → newOrganization creates fake avatar → mintReputation mints 20,000 REP → SchemeRegistrar proposes and installs scheme → sendEther moves 4.025 ETH to attacker.

### 3. Limit Break — Hack / Exploit

- **Incident date**: 2026-09-25  |  **Chains**: Ethereum  |  **Amount**: $1.70M
- **Tags**: Smart Contract Vulnerability

**Background**  
Limit Break is a platform that issues and trades NFTs. Users grant the contract operator rights over their NFTs. The Payment Processor V2 is a smart contract that handles NFT purchases. Many users had approved the contract as an operator, which the attacker later abused.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
The attacker impersonated NFT holders who had approved the contract as an operator, called the contract to buy the NFTs at a zero price, and transferred them to themselves.

**Summary**  
On Sep 25, 2026, an attacker exploited Limit Break’s Payment Processor V2 on Ethereum and bought user-approved NFTs for free. About $1.7M worth of NFTs were drained over several transactions. A white-hat recovered part of the assets and said they would be returned once the risk subsided. Limit Break is investigating the incident.

**Fund Flow**  
The stolen NFTs were transferred to the attacker’s wallet; no further movement was disclosed.
