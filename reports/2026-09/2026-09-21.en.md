### 4. Fetch.ai — Key Compromise

- **Source**: [Fetch.ai — Private Key Leakage](https://x.com/PeckShieldAlert/status/2101457227379044822) (SlowMist Hacked, 2026-09-19)
- **Incident date**: 2026-09-19  |  **Chains**: Ethereum  |  **Amount**: $1.53M
- **Tags**: Key Compromise, Private Key Compromised, Private Key Leakage
- **Other sources**: [DeFiLlama Hacks](https://defillama.com/hacks)

**Background**  
Fetch.ai operates a token-conversion contract on Ethereum. The contract relies on a bridge backend signing key to authorize conversions. The signing key was leaked, allowing an attacker to use it.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
The attacker used the leaked bridge backend signing key to call conversionIn() on the token-conversion contract, draining FET, and then used a compromised NuNet deployer key to mint unauthorized NTX.

**Summary**  
On Sep 19, 2026 the attacker used the leaked signing key to call conversionIn() on Fetch.ai’s Ethereum token-conversion contract, draining about 8,721,530 FET (~$1.53M). The same wallet cluster then used a compromised NuNet deployer key to mint roughly 408.5 M unauthorized NTX. The attack later expanded to the related ASI Alliance bridge and converter infrastructure. Recovery status is unknown.

**Fund Flow**  
FET was transferred from the conversion contract to the attacker’s wallet, and unauthorized NTX was minted to the attacker’s cluster.

### 5. Likwid — Hack / Exploit

- **Source**: [Likwid — Smart Contract Vulnerability](https://x.com/SlowMist_Team/status/2100785330849009937) (SlowMist Hacked, 2026-09-18)
- **Incident date**: 2026-09-18  |  **Chains**: BNB Chain  |  **Amount**: $55.7K
- **Tags**: Smart Contract Vulnerability
- **Follow-up — first reported**: [2026-09-20 · Likwid](https://github.com/SunWeb3Sec/DeFiHackLabs/blob/main/src/test/2026-09/Likwid_exp.sol)

**Background**  
Likwid is a DeFi protocol on BNB Chain that offers leveraged margin positions. Its LikwidMarginPosition contract contains a bug in the leverage=0 path where pool reserves are not updated. This allowed the same quote to be reused for repeated borrowing. The attacker first pumped a thin meme pool and then repeatedly executed the collateral-borrow cycle.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
The attacker exploited the smart-contract bug to borrow repeatedly without reserve updates in the leverage=0 path. After inflating the meme pool price, they cycled collateral and borrowing to drain funds.

**Summary**  
On Sep 18, 2026 the attacker leveraged the margin-contract bug to steal 74.31 BNB (≈$55.7K) from Likwid. The stolen BNB was sent to Tornado Cash, making tracing difficult. Likwid has applied a security patch and is reviewing the system to prevent further loss.

**Fund Flow**  
74.31 BNB was moved to a Tornado Cash address.

### 6. Nimiq — Hack / Exploit

- **Source**: [Nimiq — GSN forwarder skips signature check, relying solely on attacker-chosen paymaster](https://github.com/SunWeb3Sec/DeFiHackLabs/blob/main/src/test/2026-09/Nimiq_exp.sol) (DeFiHackLabs, 2026-09-16)
- **Incident date**: 2026-09-16  |  **Chains**: Polygon  |  **Amount**: $50.4K
- **Actors**: 0x2258491525C21f334c5a2dc22CE55e55023FC45D
- **Tags**: Input Validation, Signature Verification Flaw, defihacklabs
- **Follow-up — first reported**: [2026-09-18 · Nimiq](https://x.com/DefimonAlerts/status/2100868892742602793)
- **Other sources**: [DeFiLlama Hacks](https://defillama.com/hacks)
- **Known blacklist re-hits**: 4 (crimial_hunter: chainabuse_crawl, chainabuse_desc, defihacklabs, kismp_defihack)

**Background**  
Nimiq operates cross-chain HTLC handlers for swap-liquidity. The liquidity wallet granted unlimited allowances to two ERC20PermitHTLCHandler contracts. OpenGSN separates relays and paymasters to enable meta-transactions. The forwarder skips signature verification, allowing an attacker to drain tokens in the victim’s name.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
The GSN forwarder omits EIP-712 signature, nonce and business checks, and only validates the attacker-chosen paymaster, enabling token transfer from the victim.

**Summary**  
On Sep 16, 2026 the Nimiq swap-liquidity wallet lost about $50,463. The attacker deployed a malicious paymaster and used RelayHub to call open() on the victim’s address. Then a CREATE2-deployed recipient contract redeemed the HTLC with a secret, sending all tokens to the attacker. The flaw stems from the forwarder’s lack of signature verification and has not been patched yet.

**Fund Flow**  
Victim wallet → HTLC handler (forwarder) → malicious paymaster → CREATE2 recipient contract → attacker address.

### 7. Startale — Hack / Exploit

- **Source**: [Startale — transient storage init flag outlives the constructor and lets anyone reinitialize an ERC7579 account and drain it](https://github.com/SunWeb3Sec/DeFiHackLabs/blob/main/src/test/2026-09/Startale_exp.sol) (DeFiHackLabs, 2026-09-16)
- **Incident date**: 2026-09-16  |  **Chains**: Ethereum  |  **Amount**: $2.9K (427.551160 USDC drained in this batch tx (32.555561 USDC reproduced across 3 of 23 victim accounts; ~$2,876 USDC+USDT aggregate across 41 known batches))
- **Tags**: defihacklabs
- **Follow-up — first reported**: [2026-09-17 · Startale](https://x.com/exvulsec/status/2100111910524072258)
- **Known blacklist re-hits**: 2 (crimial_hunter: chainabuse_crawl, chainabuse_desc, defihacklabs, kismp_defihack, scamsniffer, tayvano_trace_extra)

**Background**  
Startale is an ERC-7579 smart-account suite on Ethereum that writes an initialization flag to transient storage during the AccountProxy constructor. The flag persists for the whole transaction, allowing re-initialization after deployment. An attacker can call initializeAccount a second time in the same tx to sweep pre-funded balances.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
The attacker exploits the transient-storage flag that outlives the constructor to re-initialize the ERC-7579 account and, via a malicious delegatecall, drains all USDC from the account.

**Summary**  
On Sep 16 2026 the attacker deployed a throwaway contract and invoked initializeAccount twice within the same transaction, draining 427.551160 USDC from 23 pre-funded accounts. The single tx caused about $427 loss, while the broader campaign has moved roughly $2,876 of USDC and USDT. DeFiHackLabs disclosed the vulnerability.

**Fund Flow**  
USDC from victim accounts → attacker contract (via delegatecall) → collector address 0xe2719E3b28EeF69bf3C4A9D7FC7280c5a015EcdE.
