### 1. MultiversX — Hack / Exploit

- **Source**: [MultiversX — VM-level atomicity vulnerability](https://x.com/multiversx/status/2101341591277391953?s=46&t=DLwbX9Nw4QECiyZQ0av-fg) (SlowMist Hacked, 2026-09-19)
- **Incident date**: 2026-09-19  |  **Chains**: MultiversX  |  **Amount**: -
- **Tags**: VM-level atomicity vulnerability

**Background**  
MultiversX is a blockchain platform that runs smart contracts on a virtual machine. The platform uses a high-performance VM but has a weakness in atomicity guarantees. The attacker tried to abuse this VM-level atomicity flaw to manipulate state. Such a flaw can break consistency when transactions are processed concurrently.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
The attacker attempted to exploit a VM-level atomicity vulnerability to create invalid on-chain state changes.

**Summary**  
On Sep 19, 2026 an attacker tried to exploit a VM-level atomicity flaw on the MultiversX mainnet. The attempt caused invalid on-chain state changes, prompting an immediate network pause. Engineers are preparing a fix on a shadow fork and evaluating a targeted recovery that rolls back only the incident-related changes. Users were advised not to submit or rebroadcast transactions and to avoid depositing or withdrawing EGLD or ESDT via exchanges or bridges.

### 3. Likwid — Hack / Exploit

- **Source**: [Likwid — missing pairDelta update in leverage=0 margin borrow freezes AMM quote](https://github.com/SunWeb3Sec/DeFiHackLabs/blob/main/src/test/2026-09/Likwid_exp.sol) (DeFiHackLabs, 2026-09-18)
- **Incident date**: 2026-09-18  |  **Chains**: BSC  |  **Amount**: 74.31 BNB (reproduced to within 3 wei)
- **Tags**: defihacklabs

**Background**  
Likwid is a margin-lending protocol on BSC where users deposit collateral via the LikwidMarginPosition contract to borrow BNB. The contract fails to update the pairDelta field when leverage is zero, leaving the AMM pool reserves unchanged. Consequently the price quote remains static, allowing unlimited borrowing at the same price. The attacker leveraged a pre-held balance of 218.58 M TOKEN to repeatedly call addMargin.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
When leverage = 0 the contract does not set pairDelta, so the AMM price never moves; the attacker repeatedly called addMargin with maximum borrow amount to drain BNB.

**Summary**  
On Sep 18 2026 the attacker exploited the missing pairDelta update in LikwidMarginPosition on BSC. By invoking addMargin 24 times they extracted a total of 74.31 BNB from the LikwidVault. The attacker used a contract that already held 218.58 M TOKEN and the borrowed BNB was sent to the attacker’s EOA. The whole exploit was executed in a single transaction. Likwid must patch the contract to update pairDelta.

**Fund Flow**  
Attacker contract’s 218.58 M TOKEN → repeated addMargin calls → 74.31 BNB borrowed → BNB transferred to attacker EOA.

### 5. PrimeFinance — Hack / Exploit

- **Source**: [PrimeFinance — permissionless stale signed oracle report overwrite drains lending pool](https://github.com/SunWeb3Sec/DeFiHackLabs/blob/main/src/test/2026-09/PrimeFinance_exp.sol) (DeFiHackLabs, 2026-09-16)

- **Tags**: defihacklabs

**Background**  
PrimeFinance is an Aave-fork lending pool deployed on HyperEVM. It holds WHYPE as reserve and relies on a DataStreamConsumer oracle verified by Chainlink. The attacker overwrote the price feed with a stale signed report, inflating the PRFI price.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
The attacker submitted a permissionless stale signed oracle report to manipulate the price, then used a Morpho flash loan to borrow WHYPE against the over-valued PRFI collateral.

**Summary**  
On Sep 16 2026 the attacker inflated PRFI price about 52x by replaying a stale oracle report. Using a 100 WHYPE flash loan they bought PRFI, supplied it as collateral, and borrowed 425.5 WHYPE from the lending pool, draining the reserve. After repaying the flash loan they netted roughly 398.7 WHYPE.

**Fund Flow**  
Flash loan of 100 WHYPE → purchase PRFI → supply PRFI as collateral → borrow 425.5 WHYPE → reserve depletion → flash loan repayment.

### 6. Flamincome — Hack / Exploit

- **Source**: [Flamincome — permissionless Convex stakeFor inflates Strategy NAV and lets VaultYUSDT overpay on withdrawAll](https://github.com/SunWeb3Sec/DeFiHackLabs/blob/main/src/test/2026-09/Flamincome_exp.sol) (DeFiHackLabs, 2026-09-16)
- **Incident date**: 2026-09-16  |  **Chains**: Ethereum  |  **Amount**: $345.9K (~$345.9K USDT net attacker profit (gross Strategy loss ~$595K in aUSDT+USDT))
- **Tags**: defihacklabs
- **Follow-up — first reported**: [2026-09-17 · Flamincome](https://x.com/SlowMist_Team/status/2100256590716907706)

**Background**  
Flamincome is a protocol on Ethereum mainnet that runs a USDT strategy. The strategy values its Convex position at the spot virtual price. The BaseRewardPool.stakeFor function is permissionless, allowing anyone to increase the strategy’s balance. The linked Curve USDP/3CRV metapool is heavily de-pegged, causing LP units to be over-valued.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
The attacker called Convex’s stakeFor without permission to inflate the strategy’s NAV. The inflated NAV let VaultYUSDT withdrawAll over-pay USDT. The whole flow started with a fee-free Morpho Blue flash loan.

**Summary**  
On Sep 16 2026 the attacker took a fee-free USDT flash loan and deposited it into VaultYUSDT. They then used the permissionless stakeFor to artificially raise the strategy’s balance and withdrew the over-paid USDT via withdrawAll. The attacker netted about $345.9K while the strategy lost roughly $595K. The exploit contract self-destructed in the same transaction, recorded under hash 0x5ff8150482f5473bff16b4a142a98a7f72b159df5e9dd38afd90470551640d37.

**Fund Flow**  
Flash-loan USDT → VaultYUSDT deposit → Convex LP stakeFor → NAV inflation → withdrawAll USDT → flash-loan repayment.

### 7. SBA PPP — Law Enforcement

- **Source**: [The U.S. Attorney’s Office for the Western District of Missouri Joins DOJ’s Fraud Division, SBA and SBA OIG in COVID-Era Loan Takedown Targeting $245 Million in COVID Loan Fraud Nationwide](https://www.justice.gov/usao-wdmo/pr/us-attorneys-office-western-district-missouri-joins-dojs-fraud-division-sba-and-sba) (US DOJ, 2026-09-14)
- **Incident date**: 2026-09-14  |  **Chains**: unknown  |  **Amount**: $245M (PPP 사기 단속 전체 규모 — 가상자산 관련 금액 미상)
- **Actors**: Jaylen Bates, Jeffrey Christopher Benton, DeZhon Byrd, Brandon A. Garrett, D’Mario Gray, Jamie William Gray, Matthew Head, Shasta Head, Dylan Gregory Neely, Sydney R. Washington, Justin Williams, Gregory Burns, Waylen Leslie, Aviance Shepard, Shawneasha Watson, William Patrick Vogt
- **Tags**: USAO - Missouri, Western

**Background**  
The SBA’s Paycheck Protection Program (PPP) was created to aid small businesses during COVID-19. During the pandemic, many fraudsters submitted false business and payroll information to steal funds. The DOJ and SBA-OIG coordinated a nationwide crackdown with over 40 U.S. Attorney’s Offices. The effort is called “Operation No Doze.”

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
Defendants fabricated nonexistent businesses and submitted false payroll and revenue data to obtain PPP loans. Some used the proceeds for personal expenses and cryptocurrency purchases. The scheme involved identity theft and falsified documentation.

**Summary**  
On Sep 14 2026 the DOJ announced that Operation No Doze led to charges and convictions against 15 defendants, recovering over $60M in losses. The overall crackdown identified about $245M in intended fraud losses. Defendants fabricated businesses and payroll to obtain PPP funds, with some spending the money on cryptocurrency. The government is refunding recovered amounts and reallocating remaining funds.

**Fund Flow**  
PPP loan proceeds were spent on personal expenses and cryptocurrency purchases.
