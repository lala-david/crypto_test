## Today's Briefing

**Liquid Network loses $320M; 28 new incidents today**

- **[Liquid Network](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/320m-exploit-liquid-network/)** (Liquid, Sep 6) — The attacker exploited a secret transaction-validation cache identification flaw, mapping invalid data to a previously approved result and minting L-BTC without collateral, then peg-out about 4,000 BTC (≈$320M). The flaw stemmed from reusing cached approvals instead of fresh verification. About 3,400 BTC has been returned; the remaining ~600 BTC stays in the attacker’s wallet pending recovery.
- **[Tectonic](https://rekt.news/tectonic-rekt)** (Cronos·Ethereum, Aug 30) — The attacker supplied $5M as collateral, borrowed TONIC repeatedly in a circular loan 98 times, inflating its price up to 195x and borrowing $120.4M across nine pools. The loop lacked proper collateral-value checks. Cronos validators rolled back 10,961 blocks, recovering $111.2M; $9.19M on Ethereum remains unrecovered.
- **[Nesa](https://rekt.news/nesa-rekt)** (Ethereum·Cosmos, Aug 24) — A Cosmos EVM accounting bug caused underflow in locked vesting accounts and overflow in others, allowing the attacker to move roughly 257.7 M NES (≈$50M) through the Hyperlane bridge to Ethereum. The bug failed to verify balances during accounting. The attacker attempted large sales on CoW Protocol and Uniswap V4 but suffered heavy slippage, reducing realized profit.
- **[Xinbi Guarantee](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/ofac-sanctions-xinbi-cybercriminal-crypto-marketplace/)** (Tron·Ethereum·BSC, Sep 9) — OFAC sanctioned the Chinese-Telegram-based guarantee marketplace for laundering hack proceeds and operating a “Black U” coin-swap service, freezing over $52M of crypto assets. Direct victim profiling enabled wallet compromises. Sentencing is pending while authorities continue tracing the proceeds.
- **[Unknown Gnosis Safe Wallet](https://x.com/SlowMist_Team/status/2099779127662493875)** (Ethereum, Sep 15) — The attacker bypassed Router’s multicall permission, delegate-called the Safe module, injected aEthrsETH into a malicious Uniswap V4 hook pool and swapped it for rsETH before withdrawing. The exploit arose from insufficient permission checks. Approximately $7.73M was drained; no recovery has been reported yet.
- **[Chainflip](https://x.com/Chainflip/status/2099080597281145040)** (Tron, Sep 12) — By attaching arbitrary memos to already-signed validator transactions, the attacker fooled the system into treating a failed same-deposit swap as a duplicate refund, executing eight attacks that paid out $736,442.17 USDT. The memo handling logic lacked validation. $115,654.41 USDT remains safely in the vault while a patch is being prepared.
- **[ORB](https://github.com/SunWeb3Sec/DeFiHackLabs/blob/main/src/test/2026-09/ORB_exp.sol)** (BSC, Sep 11) — Using flash-loaned funds the attacker bought large amounts of ORB, then exploited a re-entrancy flaw in the refund logic to repeatedly burn LP tokens, draining the pool and stealing about 45 BNB (≈$32.6K). Absence of re-entrancy protection enabled the attack. The loan was fully repaid; 47 addresses were involved and 11 address(es) already on our blacklist.
- **Small incidents** — Long Bridge (Robinhood Chain, Sep 14) $118K WETH auto-withdrawal was halted and the vault was re-funded, no user loss; Spiral (Ethereum, Sep 14) $26.8K loss from spot-price manipulation and tx.origin bypass; OMNI404 (Ethereum, Sep 9) $5.9K loss via ERC-404 dual-interpretation bug, 4 address(es) already on our blacklist; BeatXswap (BSC, Sep 9) $63.7K net profit from flash-loan price manipulation, 3 address(es) already on our blacklist; EtherFiAtomicQueue (Ethereum, Sep 9) ≈$15.45 ETH stolen through missing solver access control, 3 address(es) already on our blacklist; OrderFactory (Ethereum, Sep 8) ≈$24.7 ETH loss due to missing order-creation verification, 1 address(es) already on our blacklist; EnsoFinance (Ethereum, Sep 9) ≈5.6 ETH loss from TWAP manipulation, 1 address(es) already on our blacklist.
- **Takeaway** — Most of the incidents this week exploited missing price or balance verification, or permission checks, allowing attackers to inflate collateral or bypass access controls. Protocols should adopt multi-source price feeds, enforce re-entrancy guards, and tighten permission validation on critical functions.

### 1. Unknown Gnosis Safe Wallet — Hack / Exploit

- **Source**: [Unknown Gnosis Safe Wallet — Smart Contract Vulnerability](https://x.com/SlowMist_Team/status/2099779127662493875) (SlowMist Hacked, 2026-09-15)
- **Incident date**: 2026-09-15  |  **Chains**: Ethereum  |  **Amount**: $7.73M
- **Tags**: Smart Contract Vulnerability

**Background**  
Gnosis Safe is a multi-signature wallet on Ethereum. The victim had a module that allowed interaction with a Router contract. The Router’s multicall function contained an authorization-bypass vulnerability that the attacker exploited.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
The attacker bypassed authorization in the Router’s multicall, used the Safe module to delegate-call malicious data, injected aEthrsETH into a malicious Uniswap V4 pool, then swapped and redeemed it as rsETH.

**Summary**  
On Sep 15, 2026 an unidentified user’s Gnosis Safe wallet on Ethereum was drained of about $7.73M. The attacker exploited an authorization bypass in the Router’s multicall function and used the Safe module to delegate-call malicious code. Funds were injected as aEthrsETH into a malicious Uniswap V4 hooked pool, then swapped and redeemed as rsETH. Kelp later placed a 24-hour pause on the address that received the stolen rsETH. No recovery amount has been reported.

**Fund Flow**  
aEthrsETH injected into a malicious Uniswap V4 pool, swapped to rsETH, and the stolen rsETH was sent to an address that Kelp paused for 24 hours.

### 2. Long Bridge — Hack / Exploit

- **Source**: [Long Bridge — Supply Chain Attack](https://x.com/Longdotsupply/status/2099743494994899052) (SlowMist Hacked, 2026-09-14)
- **Incident date**: 2026-09-14  |  **Chains**: Robinhood Chain  |  **Amount**: $118.0K
- **Tags**: Supply Chain Attack

**Background**  
Long Bridge is a custodial bridge service operating on the Robinhood Chain. It holds users' WETH and processes withdrawal requests. The supply-chain attack occurred when an external RPC node supplied incorrect withdrawal events. No smart contract or private key was directly compromised.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
A third-party RPC fed fabricated Arc withdrawal events to the keeper, causing the bridge to automatically release funds.

**Summary**  
On Sep 14, 2026, fabricated RPC events caused Long Bridge to release 46.7928 WETH (≈$118,000) from its Robinhood Chain vault. No contract or key was breached, so it was not a direct hack. The team immediately halted the keeper and rebuilt the verification logic. The vault was refilled the same day from platform revenue, and users did not lose funds.

**Fund Flow**  
WETH was automatically withdrawn from the vault, then the same amount was deposited back into the vault from platform revenue.

### 4. YamFinance — Hack / Exploit

- **Source**: [YamFinance — dormant-DAO quorum governance takeover drains legacy UMA farming contracts](https://github.com/SunWeb3Sec/DeFiHackLabs/blob/main/src/test/2026-09/YamFinance_exp.sol) (DeFiHackLabs, 2026-09-12)
- **Incident date**: 2026-09-12  |  **Chains**: Ethereum  |  **Amount**: $121.0K (~$121K (48.085 WETH + 763.101 UMA))
- **Tags**: defihacklabs

**Background**  
YamFinance is an Ethereum-based DeFi protocol managed through YAM governance tokens. Governance of legacy UMA farming contracts was held by the protocol's Timelock; however, voter inactivity left the DAO vulnerable to a quorum takeover with a small circulating stake.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
The attacker bought ~504K YAM tokens with their own capital and self-delegated the voting weight, clearing both proposal threshold and quorum in a dormant DAO without counter-votes. Through this, they set themselves as the Timelock admin, took over governance of legacy UMA farming contracts, and drained collateral via admin calls.

**Summary**  
On Sep 12, 2026 an attacker acquired ~504K YAM on the open market and passed a proposal naming their EOA as Timelock pending admin. After the 5-day timelock elapsed, the attacker claimed admin rights and transferred governance of UMAFarmingMar and UMAFarmingFeb to themselves. They extracted 48.085 WETH and 763.101 UMA (~$121K) using admin settlement and arbitrary call functions.

**Fund Flow**  
The extracted 48.085 WETH and 763 UMA were swapped and cashed out via FixedFloat for ~48.15 ETH.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**

The attacker borrowed funds from Moolah and Venus to reduce the pool's ORB balance, then drained the reserve via burnLP reentrancy to net ~44.9-45 BNB (~$32.6K). All flash loans were fully repaid at the end of the transaction.

**Fund Flow**  
Flash loans borrowed from Moolah and Venus → ORB buyout and reentrancy exploit on ORB/WBNB pool → flash loans repaid → net ~44.9-45 BNB retained by attacker.

The incident drained 2.427 WETH in the main run transaction and ~3.02 ETH across four transactions.

**Fund Flow**  
Drained WETH was extracted from the Uniswap V3 pool to the exploit contract and attacker EOA.

The attacker then burned the inflated shares to extract UNI, AAVE, and MKR, resulting in a net loss of ~5.6 ETH (5.277 ETH realized post-slippage).

**Fund Flow**  
Attacker purchased FARM using 0.683 WETH → deposited FARM into Enso strategy vault to mint inflated shares → redeemed shares for UNI, AAVE, and MKR and sold them back to WETH.

### 13. BeatXswap — Hack / Exploit

- **Source**: [BeatXswap — slot0 spot-price oracle with no TWAP guard drains vesting BTX reserves](https://github.com/SunWeb3Sec/DeFiHackLabs/blob/main/src/test/2026-09/BeatXswap_exp.sol) (DeFiHackLabs, 2026-09-09)

**Background**  
BeatXswap's LiquidityVestingConvert module is a vesting contract that pairs deposited USDT with contract-held BTX to mint PancakeSwap V3 LP positions. It priced BTX directly off the V3 pool's slot0 state without a TWAP or deviation checks, leaving it susceptible to spot price manipulation.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
The attacker exploited the vesting contract which fetched spot prices from PancakeSwap V3 slot0 without a TWAP. By dumping flash-loaned BTX to crash the spot price, the attacker triggered deposit() to force the contract to add its own BTX reserves to the LP at an inflated ratio, then extracted the liquidity via reverse swap.

**Summary**

The vesting contracts were forced to mint LP positions using their own BTX at heavily distorted tick ranges, which the attacker swept via reverse swaps. After selling the net BTX and repaying the flash loans, the attacker realized about $63.7K USDT in profit.

**Fund Flow**  
Flash loans from Moolah and Pancake Vault → BTX dumped into V3 pool → manipulated LP minting via victim contracts → reverse swap to reclaim BTX → ~2.98M BTX sold on normal CL pool for ~77,512 USDT → loans repaid with ~63,704 USDT kept.
