### 1. Nostra — Hack / Exploit

- **Source**: [Nostra — Oracle Manipulation](https://x.com/nostrafinance/status/2100577538053493076?s=46&t=DLwbX9Nw4QECiyZQ0av-fg) (SlowMist Hacked, 2026-09-17)
- **Incident date**: 2026-09-17  |  **Chains**: Starknet, Ethereum  |  **Amount**: $3.50M
- **Tags**: Oracle Manipulation, Spot Price Manipulation
- **Other sources**: [DeFiLlama Hacks](https://defillama.com/hacks)

**Background**  
Nostra is a DeFi lending protocol built on Starknet. Users can deposit NSTR as collateral and borrow assets such as ETH, STRK, USDC, USDT, WBTC and DAI. The protocol relies on an NSTR price oracle for valuation. When the oracle price was manipulated, the attacker obtained excessive collateral.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
The attacker inflated the NSTR oracle price to create over-collateralization and then borrowed against it.

**Summary**  
On Sep 17, 2026 the attacker used the manipulated NSTR price as collateral to borrow about $3.5M of assets. The borrowed assets included ETH, STRK, USDC, USDT, WBTC and DAI. Nostra paused the market and began tracing the funds. About $1.92M has already been bridged to Ethereum, and the final loss and recovery amounts are still unconfirmed.

**Fund Flow**  
$1.92M of the borrowed funds was bridged to Ethereum.

### 4. RsETHSafeModule — Hack / Exploit

- **Source**: [RsETHSafeModule — nested self-referential multicall authorization bypass drains Gnosis Safe via DELEGATECALL](https://github.com/SunWeb3Sec/DeFiHackLabs/blob/main/src/test/2026-09/RsETHSafeModule_exp.sol) (DeFiHackLabs, 2026-09-15)
- **Incident date**: 2026-09-15  |  **Chains**: Ethereum  |  **Amount**: $7.80M (2,900 aEthrsETH (~$7.8M gross; 2,882.37 rsETH / ~$7.73M net to the MEV frontrunner))
- **Actors**: 0x0dC2c5D6b05A317076CF501f7E7be36a5dfe9b66, 0x2f7e143e27F2fa26Ef3B8AC72698F1D321422f67, 0xFDe0d1575Ed8E06FBf36256bcdfA1F359281455A
- **Tags**: defihacklabs
- **Follow-up — first reported**: [2026-09-15 · Unknown Gnosis Safe Wallet](https://x.com/SlowMist_Team/status/2099779127662493875)
- **Known blacklist re-hits**: 4 (crimial_hunter: chainabuse_crawl, defi_rekt, defihacklabs, kismp_defihack)

**Background**  
RsETHSafeModule is a private DeFi-Saver-style router that holds rsETH collateral in a Gnosis Safe. The router exposes a multicall(address,bytes[]) function that authorizes calls when the target equals the router itself. This design lets an external caller wrap the router call in a self-referential multicall and gain trusted identity. Consequently, the router can forward calls to an enabled Safe module without owner signatures.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
The attacker nested a self-referential multicall, then called the enabled module which used DELEGATECALL to execute a recipe inside the Safe, draining the aEthrsETH.

**Summary**  
On Sep 15, 2026 the attacker submitted transaction 0x0e7680… and drained 2,900 aEthrsETH from the victim Safe. The victim Safe (0x40E93a…) originally held 53,402 aEthrsETH, which were completely removed. The stolen tokens were sent to MEV frontrunner address 0xFDe0…, yielding 2,882.37 rsETH (~$7.73M) profit. The exploit relied on an unauthorized nested multicall and a DELEGATECALL to the Safe.

**Fund Flow**  
The attacker’s tx 0x0e7680… moved the aEthrsETH from the Safe to the MEV frontrunner address 0xFDe0….

### 6. SBA Paycheck Protection Program — Law Enforcement

- **Source**: [Eastern District of Virginia joins DOJ Fraud Division, SBA, and SBA OIG in surge takedown exceeding $245M in COVID-era loan fraud](https://www.justice.gov/usao-edva/pr/eastern-district-virginia-joins-doj-fraud-division-sba-and-sba-oig-surge-takedown) (US DOJ, 2026-09-14)
- **Incident date**: unknown  |  **Chains**: unknown  |  **Amount**: $245.00M ($245 million)
- **Actors**: Karen J. DeMatteo, Tae Young Q. Jang, Sheila Bynum-Coleman
- **Tags**: National Fraud Enforcement Division, USAO - Virginia, Eastern
- **Follow-up — first reported**: [2026-09-16 · Select Decks / Vin Phat](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ndwv/pr/northern-district-west-virginia-joins-doj-fraud-division-sba-and-sba-oig-surge)

**Background**  
The Paycheck Protection Program (PPP) provided payroll assistance to small businesses during the COVID-19 pandemic. Federal authorities issued loans through the program, and fraudsters submitted false business and payroll information. This enforcement action was a joint effort by the DOJ and SBA to combat large-scale fraud.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
Defendants created fictitious businesses and inflated payroll and revenue figures to obtain loans. They also used identity theft and concealed foreign ties to misappropriate funds.

**Summary**  
On Sep 14, 2026, the Eastern District of Virginia announced a $245M PPP fraud takedown involving over 160 defendants. Key defendants caused losses of $2.125M, $225K and $17K respectively. Misappropriated funds were used to buy cryptocurrency, real estate and a car. Courts sentenced the defendants to prison, and the government is pursuing fund recovery and prevention measures.

**Fund Flow**  
PPP/EIDL loan proceeds were spent on cryptocurrency purchases, housing and vehicle acquisitions.
