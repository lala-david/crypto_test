### 2. Blockchain Dead Drops (BDD) — Other

- **Source**: [DPRK and Iran are Leading a 5.2x Surge YoY in Blockchain-Assisted Cyberattacks](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/etherhiding-blockchain-dead-drops/) (Chainalysis, 2026-09-17)
- **Incident date**: unknown  |  **Chains**: Bitcoin, Binance Smart Chain, TRON, Aptos, Polygon, Ethereum  |  **Amount**: -
- **Actors**: UNC5342, Iran Ministry of Intelligence, Russian-language cybercriminal groups
- **Tags**: Blockchain dead drops, Crime, Crypto crime, Cybercrime, Etherhiding, Malware

**Background**  
Blockchain dead drops (BDD) store malware commands or payloads on public blockchains, creating takedown-resistant infrastructure. State-backed actors have adopted this technique to maintain persistent C2 channels. Since 2023 the practice has spread across BSC, TRON, Aptos and other chains. Recently North Korean, Iranian and Russian-language groups are the main users.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
Threat actors embed C2 data in transaction fields such as Bitcoin OP_RETURN or in smart-contract state. Some create cross-chain relays that encode pointers on TRON and Aptos which resolve to a BSC transaction. Others hide IP information in phantom wallets that have no private keys.

**Summary**  
Chainalysis identified over 15 campaigns using BDD techniques. A DPRK-linked group in 2025 employed a TRON-Aptos-BSC relay to deliver malicious payloads. Iranian-linked actors write C2 information to Bitcoin OP_RETURN fields. Russian-language cybercriminals run C2 resolvers on Polygon smart contracts. These methods are hard to block and pose a lasting threat.

- **Source**: [Eastern District of Virginia joins DOJ Fraud Division, SBA, and SBA OIG in surge takedown exceeding $245M in COVID-era loan fraud](https://www.justice.gov/usao-edva/pr/eastern-district-virginia-joins-doj-fraud-division-sba-and-sba-oig-surge-takedown) (US DOJ, 2026-09-14)
- **Incident date**: unknown  |  **Chains**: unknown  |  **Amount**: $245.00M ($245 million)
- **Actors**: Karen J. DeMatteo, Tae Young Q. Jang, Sheila Bynum-Coleman
- **Tags**: National Fraud Enforcement Division, USAO - Virginia, Eastern

**Background**  
The Paycheck Protection Program (PPP) provided payroll assistance to small businesses during the COVID-19 pandemic. Federal authorities issued loans through the program, and fraudsters submitted false business and payroll information. This enforcement action was a joint effort by the DOJ and SBA to combat large-scale fraud.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
Defendants created fictitious businesses and inflated payroll and revenue figures to obtain loans. They also used identity theft and concealed foreign ties to misappropriate funds.

**Summary**  
On Sep 14, 2026, the Eastern District of Virginia announced a $245M PPP fraud takedown involving over 160 defendants. Key defendants caused losses of $2.125M, $225K and $17K respectively. Misappropriated funds were used to buy cryptocurrency, real estate and a car. Courts sentenced the defendants to prison, and the government is pursuing fund recovery and prevention measures.

**Fund Flow**  
PPP/EIDL loan proceeds were spent on cryptocurrency purchases, housing and vehicle acquisitions.
