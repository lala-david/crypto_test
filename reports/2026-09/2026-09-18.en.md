### 5. RsETHSafeModule — Hack / Exploit

- **Source**: [RsETHSafeModule — nested self-referential multicall authorization bypass drains Gnosis Safe via DELEGATECALL](https://github.com/SunWeb3Sec/DeFiHackLabs/blob/main/src/test/2026-09/RsETHSafeModule_exp.sol) (DeFiHackLabs, 2026-09-15)
- **Incident date**: 2026-09-15  |  **Chains**: Ethereum  |  **Amount**: $7.80M (2,900 aEthrsETH (~$7.8M gross; 2,882.37 rsETH / ~$7.73M net to the MEV frontrunner))
- **Actors**: 0x0dC2c5D6b05A317076CF501f7E7be36a5dfe9b66, 0x2f7e143e27F2fa26Ef3B8AC72698F1D321422f67, 0xFDe0d1575Ed8E06FBf36256bcdfA1F359281455A
- **Tags**: defihacklabs
- **Follow-up — first reported**: [2026-09-15 · Unknown Gnosis Safe Wallet](https://x.com/SlowMist_Team/status/2099779127662493875)
- **Known blacklist re-hits**: 4 (crimial_hunter: chainabuse_crawl, defi_rekt, defihacklabs, kismp_defihack)

**Background**  
RsETHSafeModule is a private DeFi-Saver-style router that holds rsETH collateral in a Gnosis Safe. The router exposes a multicall(address,bytes[]) function that authorizes calls when the target equals the router itself. This design lets an external caller wrap the router call in a self-referential multicall and gain trusted identity. Consequently, the router can forward calls to an enabled Safe module without owner signatures.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
The attacker nested a self-referential multicall, then called the enabled module which used DELEGATECALL to execute a recipe inside the Safe, draining the aEthrsETH.

**Summary**  
On Sep 15, 2026 the attacker submitted transaction 0x0e7680… and drained 2,900 aEthrsETH from the victim Safe. The victim Safe (0x40E93a…) originally held 53,402 aEthrsETH, which were completely removed. The stolen tokens were sent to MEV frontrunner address 0xFDe0…, yielding 2,882.37 rsETH (~$7.73M) profit. The exploit relied on an unauthorized nested multicall and a DELEGATECALL to the Safe.

**Fund Flow**  
The attacker’s tx 0x0e7680… moved the aEthrsETH from the Safe to the MEV frontrunner address 0xFDe0….
