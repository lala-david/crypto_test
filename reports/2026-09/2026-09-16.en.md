### 2. Fake AI Trading Bot Drainer — Phishing / Social Eng.

- **Source**: [Fake AI Trading Bots Are Getting Victims to Build Their Own Drainers](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/fake-ai-trading-bots-are-getting-victims-to-build-their-own-drainers) (TRM Labs, 2026-09-14)
- **Incident date**: 2026-02-12  |  **Chains**: Ethereum  |  **Amount**: $517.2K

**Background**  
The scam posts YouTube tutorials claiming to teach how to build an AI-powered crypto arbitrage bot. Viewers are instructed to connect a wallet, copy code, and compile it on a malicious compiler site. The compiler discards the submitted source and replaces it with malicious bytecode that forwards any deposited ETH to the operators. Because victims deploy and fund the contract themselves, standard wallet warnings do not trigger.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
Victims follow the tutorial to deploy a smart contract and fund it from their own wallet. The malicious compiler substitutes the code with a drainer contract that automatically sends the received ETH to the attacker’s address.

**Summary**  
From February to August 2026, 224 victims lost a total of 274.60 ETH (≈ $517,205) to six operator addresses. Victims deployed contracts themselves, so the funds moved only through decentralized finance, cross-chain bridges and a mixer, with no centralized exchange involved. The lack of phishing links or approval prompts allowed the attack to bypass typical wallet defenses. TRM identified nine near-identical tutorials and traced the on-chain flows in real time. The operator addresses remain active on-chain.

**Fund Flow**  
Funds flow from victim-deployed contracts to six operator wallets, then some are swapped to DAI via DeFi, and later routed through multiple bridges and a mixer to other chains.

### 4. Select Decks / Vin Phat — Law Enforcement

- **Source**: [The Northern District of West Virginia joins DOJ Fraud Division, SBA, and SBA OIG in Surge Takedown Exceeding $245 Million in COVID-era Loan Fraud; Two Morgantown Business Owners Part of National Fraud Takedown](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ndwv/pr/northern-district-west-virginia-joins-doj-fraud-division-sba-and-sba-oig-surge) (US DOJ, )
- **Incident date**: unknown  |  **Chains**: unknown  |  **Amount**: $245.00M ($245 million)
- **Actors**: James Baldwin, Kurt Ly
- **Tags**: USAO - West Virginia, Northern

**Background**  
The Paycheck Protection Program (PPP) was created to provide payroll assistance to small businesses during the COVID-19 pandemic. Federal prosecutors have uncovered schemes that used falsified documents and stolen identities to obtain loans fraudulently.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
Defendants fabricated businesses, inflated payroll and revenue figures, and stole identities to secure PPP and EIDL loans.

**Summary**  
From June 12 to September 1, a coordinated effort by 40 U.S. Attorney’s Offices targeted over 160 defendants, uncovering approximately $245M in alleged fraud. Morgantown entrepreneur James Baldwin received a 30-day prison sentence and $1,007,223 in restitution. Vin Phat owner Kurt Ly was charged for misusing PPP and EIDL funds on personal expenses, including a vehicle purchase and cryptocurrency investments. Officials called the operation the largest PPP fraud takedown to date.

**Fund Flow**  
Ly transferred PPP and EIDL proceeds to a personal bank account, used $15,000 for a vehicle, and invested in cryptocurrency.
