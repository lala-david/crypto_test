### 5. SpiralHookV2 — Hack / Exploit

- **Source**: [SpiralHookV2 — Uniswap V4 spot-price borrow plus tx.origin-keyed same-block guard bypass](https://github.com/SunWeb3Sec/DeFiHackLabs/blob/main/src/test/2026-09/SpiralHookV2_exp.sol) (DeFiHackLabs, 2026-09-12)
- **Incident date**: 2026-09-12  |  **Chains**: Ethereum  |  **Amount**: ~10.70728 ETH (reproduced exact to the wei)
- **Actors**: 0x859E69A29244A10800A34eE66919426C02afa2F0
- **Tags**: defihacklabs
- **Follow-up — first reported**: [2026-09-15 · Spiral](https://x.com/SlowMist_Team/status/2099410818244968850)
- **Known blacklist re-hits**: 1 (crimial_hunter: chainabuse_crawl, kismp_defihack)

**Background**  
SpiralHookV2 is a hook-based lending market built on Uniswap V4 pools. It allows borrowing ETH using the SPIRAL token as collateral. Collateral valuation reads the live spot price from the pool manager without a TWAP, and the noSameBlockSwap guard checks tx.origin to block same-block swaps.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
The attacker deposited 54 ETH into the pool to buy SPIRAL, inflating the spot price. Six different EOAs then called the same contract to borrow ETH against the over-valued SPIRAL, each with a fresh tx.origin that bypassed the guard.

**Summary**  
On Sep 12 2026 the exploit was executed within a single block (25974146). Attacker EOA 0x859E… pumped 54 ETH into the pool, pushing the SPIRAL price to about 0.0012041 ETH per token. Six subsequent borrows through distinct EOAs withdrew a total of 64.7072 ETH, leaving a net protocol loss of roughly 10.7072 ETH. The incident highlights the risk of spot-price oracles without TWAP and tx.origin-based defenses.

**Fund Flow**  
Attacker EOA → 54 ETH deposited (price pump) → 6 EOAs each borrow ETH using SPIRAL collateral → 64.7072 ETH leaves the protocol, net loss 10.7072 ETH.

### 6. Fake AI Trading Bot Drainer — Phishing / Social Eng.

- **Source**: [Fake AI Trading Bots Are Getting Victims to Build Their Own Drainers](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/fake-ai-trading-bots-are-getting-victims-to-build-their-own-drainers) (TRM Labs, 2026-09-14)
- **Incident date**: 2026-02-12  |  **Chains**: Ethereum  |  **Amount**: $517.2K

**Background**  
The scam posts YouTube tutorials claiming to teach how to build an AI-powered crypto arbitrage bot. Viewers are instructed to connect a wallet, copy code, and compile it on a malicious compiler site. The compiler discards the submitted source and replaces it with malicious bytecode that forwards any deposited ETH to the operators. Because victims deploy and fund the contract themselves, standard wallet warnings do not trigger.

**Attack / Modus Operandi**  
Victims follow the tutorial to deploy a smart contract and fund it from their own wallet. The malicious compiler substitutes the code with a drainer contract that automatically sends the received ETH to the attacker’s address.

**Summary**  
From February to August 2026, 224 victims lost a total of 274.60 ETH (≈ $517,205) to six operator addresses. Victims deployed contracts themselves, so the funds moved only through decentralized finance, cross-chain bridges and a mixer, with no centralized exchange involved. The lack of phishing links or approval prompts allowed the attack to bypass typical wallet defenses. TRM identified nine near-identical tutorials and traced the on-chain flows in real time. The operator addresses remain active on-chain.

**Fund Flow**  
Funds flow from victim-deployed contracts to six operator wallets, then some are swapped to DAI via DeFi, and later routed through multiple bridges and a mixer to other chains.
